to genepatt...@googlegroups.com
Matthew,


yesterday there were three accounts created with disposable email addresses; deseqaccount1, deseqaccount2 and deseqaccount3.  I presume that all three are yours.  As a result of all three accounts simultaneously hitting our server with many more jobs than the normal quota of 20, all other GenePattern users were blocked from running any jobs for several hours.  In order to allow other users access to the server, we had to block these accounts.  In effect they were acting like a denial of service attack even though we know that was not the intent. 


Creating multiple accounts to get around job submission limitations is against our terms of service and affects our ability to provide services to our other users. We will therefore limit or terminate these accounts when we identify them.


I look forward to being able to accommodate all of these requests when we have the XSEDE resources available from GenePattern's  main server. 


Ted


p.s. Some nitty-gritty details.  


The problem was exacerbated because of your DeSeq jobs was requesting 16 CPU and 128 GB of RAM which contributed to the problem since with that level of resources required, it is not possible to execute multiple jobs on each compute node that we launch.  Particularly because of the high memory, each of your DeSeq jobs required an M5.12xlarge (48 cpu, 192GB RAM) or bigger node to itself.  Our configuration limits GenePattern to a max of 256 CPU at any given moment in order to prevent the cost to our lab from exploding and preventing us from continuing to provide this service to the community for free.  With this level of resources for each job it meant that at most we could launch 5 (very large) compute nodes at one time.  Since each of your jobs took ~40 minutes to run, your 3 accounts hitting us in parallel would consume all available GenePattern resources for 8 hours each time they submitted 20 jobs each.  This is what prevented any other GenePattern users from running any jobs at all for most of yesterday.


I am not familiar with DeSeq2's resource requirements, but if it is possible to run it with smaller resources (especially memory) that could make a huge difference.
