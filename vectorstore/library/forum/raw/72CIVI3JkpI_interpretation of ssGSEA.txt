Hi Mohamed,

 

ssGSEA scores can be positive or negative, in the case of your data here, it looks like there is at least moderate enrichment of the EMT signature in all of your samples (although one of them, the third one I think, looks quite highly enriched). I’ve attached a screenshot of the a quick plotting of the enrichment scores of your samples. You can see that, with some exceptions, they’re all pretty similar.

If you want to determine unbiased cutoffs for calling Mesenchymal, or Epithelial types from the enrichment scores, you’d need biological controls to compare your sample’s enrichment scores against.

 

The GMT contains only the gene symbols, not information on where the genes “should be” in any given expression dataset. ssGSEA uses the expression values from your input dataset to determine if the genes are highly expressed or lowly expressed in your samples and determines a score on aggregate if those genes appear towards the top or the bottom of the sample’s gene expression.

Hi Mohamed,

 

No, ssGSEA uses the expression values of the genes in the gene set as well as the relative position of these genes in the ranked list to calculate the enrichment score. In order to determine their relative positions in the expression list ssGSEA needs the information from all genes expressed in the sample. There isn’t anything to unmask, looking at the relative position of the genes is an essential component of the enrichment score calculation. ssGSEA doesn’t use the actual expression values from the genes that aren’t in the gene set, it just uses their presence and position to determine where in the list the genes of interest are.

 

For controls, you could try to use publicly available data but you’d want to make sure that at minimum, they’ve been normalized the same way, and, ideally quantified using the same pipeline. It’s hard to say how well it’ll work without giving it a try.
