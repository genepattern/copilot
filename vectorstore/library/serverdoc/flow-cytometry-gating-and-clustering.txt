Flow cytometry is a technique for analyzing individual cells based on their physical and fluorescent properties.
Gating is the process of selecting specific cell populations from flow cytometry data by defining regions based on parameter values.
Manual gating involves a researcher visually selecting and defining cell populations, while automatic gating uses computational algorithms to identify cell subsets.
Clustering algorithms like FlowClust, FlowMeans, and FLOCK aim to objectively identify cell populations without subjective human intervention.
Different clustering methods use various approaches, such as model-based clustering, density-based methods, and spectral clustering techniques.
Clustering algorithms can identify cell populations by analyzing multiple dimensions of data, including physical characteristics and fluorescence intensity.
The number of cell populations can be estimated using statistical methods like the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) and Integrated Completed Likelihood (ICL).
Meta-clustering techniques allow comparison of cell populations across different samples by matching cluster centroids.
Feature extraction from gated populations typically includes counting cells, calculating percentages, and measuring mean fluorescence intensity.
Automated gating methods can help researchers identify novel cell subpopulations and reduce the labor-intensive process of manual gating.
Different clustering algorithms have strengths in handling various data characteristics, such as non-spherical populations or large datasets.
Cross-sample population identification helps researchers compare cell populations across different experimental conditions or patient samples.
