The ESPPredictor is a computational method for predicting peptide performance in mass spectrometry assays
The method was developed by Fusaro et al. in a 2009 Nature Biotechnology publication
The module predicts which peptides from a protein sequence are most likely to work well in targeted protein assays
Users must provide a text file containing peptide sequences, with one sequence per line
The prediction is based solely on the peptide sequence information
The module can process small numbers of peptides quickly (less than 20) or large numbers over many hours
The prediction output includes each peptide sequence and its probability of successful assay performance
Common errors include non-standard amino acids or invisible characters in the input file
Users can access the module through the GenePattern public server at http://genepattern.broadinstitute.org/gp/
A login or registration is required to use the GenePattern platform
The module is categorized under the Proteomics section of available tools
After submission, users can track job status and retrieve results from the Job Results page
The prediction.txt file contains the final computational assessment of peptide performance probabilities
Users can continue working or exit GenePattern while the analysis is processing
The method represents an computational approach to optimizing mass spectrometry peptide selection
Peptide performance prediction can help researchers design more effective targeted protein assays
The tool is particularly useful for researchers working with proteomics and mass spectrometry techniques
